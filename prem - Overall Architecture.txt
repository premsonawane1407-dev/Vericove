 idea is essentially a healthcare-focused, anonymous Reddit-style community where:
	• Women can discuss similar health conditions and symptoms.
	• Patients can share their healthcare journeys and treatment experiences.
	• Doctors can participate in discussions without publicly revealing their personal identities.
	• People can ask questions and receive responses from others with relevant experiences.
	• Patients can discover discussions related to their health concerns.
	• The community can help people feel less isolated when dealing with unexplained or misunderstood health concerns.
There is one important distinction: sharing treatment experiences is different from recommending treatments. The interface should encourage people to share what happened in their own experience, without presenting individual experiences as medical advice.
Let's rebuild the entire application architecture around this addition.

E. Anonymous Identity System
This is a major architectural feature, not simply a username generator.
I recommend three public identity types:

The user can interact under a public pseudonym without displaying their actual identity.
For doctors, credentials can be verified by the platform while their personal identity remains hidden from ordinary community members.
Important privacy distinction: Anonymous to other users does not mean that the platform has no identity records. The platform needs protected account records for security, abuse prevention and moderation. Explain this clearly, minimise collected data, and never promise absolute anonymity.

G. Community Moderation & Safety
This is especially important because people will be sharing sensitive medical experiences.
Include:
	• Automated spam and harmful-content detection.
	• Report and flag functionality.
	• Human moderation.
	• Verified professional identification internally.
	• Medical misinformation warnings.
	• Restrictions on sharing identifiable medical records.
	• Protection against harassment and targeted accusations.
	• Community-specific guidelines.
	• Appeal and moderation review mechanisms.
Do not allow the community to become a place where users diagnose each other or prescribe medications. It should facilitate peer support and experience sharing.
3. How all five modules connect
The five modules should not function as isolated destinations.
Here is the relationship I recommend:
The interconnected ecosystem

The most interesting connections are:
	1. Symptom Intelligence → Community: users can discover discussions related to topics they choose to follow, without automatically exposing their private health records.
	2. Community → Healthcare Directory: discussions can help users discover what to consider when choosing a healthcare provider.
	3. Community → Research Hub: interesting scientific questions raised in discussions can lead users to relevant publications.
	4. Symptom Intelligence → Researcher Matching: user-approved health summaries can help identify relevant research topics.
	5. Research Hub → Community: research articles can be shared in community discussions, with their scientific context preserved.
One rule should govern all five connections: private health information never moves between modules automatically. Sharing must be explicit, understandable and revocable where appropriate.

6. Suggested development priorities
Not every feature needs to be built immediately. I would prioritise the following:
Module	MVP features	Later additions
Symptom Intelligence	Journal, records, basic AI summary	Advanced pattern analysis
Healthcare Directory	Doctor search, profiles, reviews	Advanced verification and filters
Researcher Matching	Literature search, researcher matches	Advanced matching and study discovery
Research Hub	Publication feed, article details	Research collaboration and gap analysis
Community	Anonymous posts, comments, communities, reporting	Messaging, advanced recommendations
For the Community MVP, the minimum viable experience should include:
	1. Anonymous registration and pseudonyms.
	2. Condition-based communities.
	3. Create posts and discussions.
	4. Comments and replies.
	5. Upvotes or helpful reactions.
	6. Verified professional badges.
	7. Report and moderation functionality.
	8. Community guidelines.
I would prioritise this over anonymous private messaging, which can be introduced later.
7. Final architectural decisions
There are three particularly important design decisions I would make now.
1. Community and Research Hub must remain separate.
Community	Research Hub
Experience-driven	Evidence-driven
Patients and professionals	Researchers, doctors and public
Anonymous discussions	Attributed publications
Questions and personal stories	Scientific findings
Peer support	Scientific knowledge exchange
Community moderation	Research verification
They can link to each other, but they should not share the same feed or content structure.
2. Use a shared identity system with role-based permissions.
A user might be a patient, doctor or researcher. A verified doctor can also participate in the community anonymously. This should not require creating a second account.
3. Build privacy into the application architecture.
Health records, community identities, public discussions and professional verification data should have separate access permissions. Do not expose patient identities through posts, comments, metadata or uploaded files.
8. What I would consider the final product vision



My final recommendation: Make these five modules the permanent pillars of the product. The Community should be treated as equally important as the other four, because it provides something the other modules cannot: a human connection between people experiencing similar healthcare challenges.
It also creates a particularly interesting product loop:
Personal experiences → Structured health information → Community knowledge → Scientific research → Better-informed healthcare conversations.
