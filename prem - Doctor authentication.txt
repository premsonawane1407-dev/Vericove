a dedicated Doctor Credential Verification System rather than simply asking doctors to upload their degrees and trusting the submitted documents.
This is particularly important because your platform will allow doctors to:
	• Create professional profiles.
	• Participate in anonymous health discussions.
	• Share medical information.
	• Receive a verified professional badge.
	• Potentially influence patients' healthcare decisions.
A fake doctor or someone misrepresenting their qualifications could seriously undermine the platform's credibility.
My recommendation is to introduce a proper verification workflow involving document submission, official registry checks and manual administrator approval.
1. What exactly should we verify?
We should separate a doctor's identity, professional registration, education and additional achievements.
Verification category	What to verify
Identity	Whether the applicant is the person submitting the application
Medical registration	Valid registration with the relevant medical council
Basic qualification	MBBS or applicable recognised primary qualification
Postgraduate degree	MD, MS, DNB or other applicable qualification
Specialisation	Whether the claimed speciality is supported by recognised qualifications
Additional qualifications	Fellowships, diplomas and certifications
Professional experience	Employment history and institutional affiliations
Achievements	Publications, awards, research and professional contributions
Important distinction: A doctor's identity being verified does not automatically mean their qualifications, specialisation or achievements have been verified. Each should have its own verification status.
2. How to verify doctors in India
Since your initial target appears to be India, I would use the following official verification sources.
The first step is to identify the doctor's medical system. For an Indian allopathic doctor, the primary official sources are the National Medical Commission (NMC), National Medical Register (NMR) and relevant State Medical Council.






The NMC's National Medical Register is particularly useful because its registration system also supports additional qualification registration. The NMR FAQ explains that doctors can apply to register additional qualifications after their primary registration. 

NMC+1
For other healthcare systems, do not use an MBBS/NMC verification workflow indiscriminately. For example, dental, nursing and AYUSH professionals have different regulatory frameworks. Your platform should have separate verification rules for each professional category.
3. The verification workflow I recommend
I would make the process a combination of automated checks and human verification.






There should also be a status for pending verification. Doctors must not receive a verified badge simply because they submitted documents.
4. How to verify each degree and qualification
This is where the system needs to be particularly careful.
Imagine a doctor submits:
	• MBBS – XYZ Medical College.
	• MD – General Medicine.
	• Fellowship in Endocrinology.
	• 12 years of experience.
	• Published 15 research papers.
	• Awarded Best Doctor Award in 2024.
We should not verify all of these using one single document or badge.
Instead, create a separate verification record for every claim.
Claim	Verification method
MBBS	Degree certificate + official registration records
MD	Degree certificate + recognised qualification and institution checks
Fellowship	Issuing institution and programme verification
Specialisation	Relevant recognised qualification and registration records
Work experience	Employer or institution confirmation
Research publications	DOI, PubMed or publisher records
Awards	Awarding organisation and official evidence
Professional memberships	Membership issuer confirmation
A. Degree verification
For every submitted degree:
	1. Doctor enters the qualification name.
	2. Selects the awarding university or institution.
	3. Enters graduation year.
	4. Uploads the certificate.
	5. Platform checks the qualification against applicable recognised qualification records.
	6. Admin verifies the certificate and, where necessary, contacts the issuing institution.
	7. The qualification receives an individual verification status.
For additional qualifications, also check whether the doctor has registered them with the relevant council where applicable.
B. University verification
Do not rely only on the uploaded degree certificate.
Use a combination of:
	• Official university websites.
	• University verification departments.
	• Official academic verification services, where available.
	• Direct confirmation from the institution.
	• Relevant medical regulator qualification records.
For suspicious or inconsistent documents, require additional evidence or direct institutional confirmation.
C. Achievement verification
Achievements should have their own verification process.
For example:
Achievement	How to verify
Research publication	DOI and publisher record
Research paper	PubMed or journal website
Conference speaker	Official conference programme
Medical award	Awarding organisation
Fellowship	Issuing institution
Professional membership	Professional organisation
Hospital employment	Hospital or employer confirmation
A publication appearing on PubMed can establish that a publication record exists, but it does not independently establish every authorship or professional claim. Those need appropriate checks too.

8. Technical implementation: how we actually build it
For your development team, I recommend the following architecture.





Suggested database structure
Don't store all verification information in a single doctors table.
Use separate entities:
Table	Purpose
users	Login and account details
doctor_profiles	Professional profile
professional_registrations	Council, number, validity and status
qualifications	Degrees and educational history
qualification_evidence	Uploaded certificates
achievements	Awards, publications and memberships
achievement_evidence	Supporting documents and references
verification_checks	Verification results
verification_audit_logs	Who checked what and when
professional_badges	Publicly displayed verified statuses
Each verification record should contain:
	• Claim being verified.
	• Evidence reference.
	• Source of verification.
	• Verification date.
	• Reviewer.
	• Result.
	• Expiration or recheck date, if applicable.
Important implementation consideration
Do not assume that NMC or university databases provide a publicly accessible API for your application.
For your first version, use manual official-registry checks and administrator review. If an authorised API or institutional integration becomes available, automate the relevant checks later.
Also:
	• Encrypt uploaded certificates.
	• Restrict access to authorised verification personnel.
	• Keep private identity documents separate from public profile data.
	• Do not publicly display Aadhaar numbers or full registration documents.
	• Record every administrative change.
	• Provide a correction and appeal process.
9. One more thing: continuous verification
Doctor verification should not be a one-time event.
Suppose a doctor is approved today, but their registration subsequently expires or is suspended.
Your platform needs to account for that.
I recommend these statuses:
Status	Meaning
Pending	Application submitted
Under Review	Verification underway
Verified	Required checks completed
Partially Verified	Some qualifications remain unverified
Action Required	Additional evidence needed
Expired	Registration validity needs confirmation
Suspended	Relevant regulatory suspension confirmed
Rejected	Verification unsuccessful
The system should periodically recheck professional registration and provide a mechanism for doctors to update their qualifications.
